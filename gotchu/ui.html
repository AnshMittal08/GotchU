Look for signs of infection

Most people never find out they were attacked. This looks at what is running and what starts with Windows, and tells you if anything behaves the way malware does.

  • Programs pretending to be part of Windows, or hiding inside a Windows helper
  • Programs running from the Temp folder or a malware-style drop folder
  • Trusted programs loading an untrusted DLL placed beside them
  • Hidden or admin-level tasks and start-up entries
  • Scripts and programs hiding in the Start-up folder
  • Web traffic redirected through a proxy, protection switched off, extensions you cannot remove

What a thief could take

Password-stealing malware looks in the same few places on every PC. This looks in those places the way it would, and counts what is there. It never opens, copies or shows a password or key.

  • Passwords and signed-in sessions saved in your browsers
  • Chat apps that stay signed in, such as Telegram and Discord
  • Cloud and developer logins, key files and recovery codes
  • Crypto wallets

Lock down this PC

Windows has strong protections that are switched off by default. Each one below says what it stops and what it costs you. gotchu saves the old setting before any change, so you can always put it back.

Tripwire

Decoy files that no honest program opens. If something reads one, you see a warning window at once. Programs other than the owner that read your browser or chat logins are listed here for you to review.

To switch it on or off, or lift an internet block after an alarm, use the command line: gotchu tripwire --on, --off or --restore.
